About

Bonjour! I’m Gaëlle — welcome to my corner of the internet.

I hold a Ph.D. in Public Health from the University of California, Irvine, where my dissertation focused on the ecology and forecasting of invasive Aedes aegypti populations in Southern California. I'm now a postdoctoral researcher at the Institut für Epidemiologie und Sozialmedizin, Universitätsklinikum Münster (UKM), where I work within the VBD-MODE consortium on modeling tick-borne encephalitis (TBE) risk across Germany.

I was born and raised in Côte d’Ivoire, and my background has shaped the way I think about health, inequality, and the importance of context in public health research. I studied biochemistry and biology at the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee, where I built a strong foundation in the life sciences.

After graduation, I worked in a lithium battery startup laboratory before deciding to pursue an MPH in Epidemiology. During that time, I contributed to a range of public health projects, including research on breast cancer risk factors in Kathmandu, Nepal, to support the development of a culturally tailored survey instrument, and survey data analysis in the Dominican Republic to examine factors associated with teen dating violence among students.

These experiences strengthened my interest in global and environmental health and in using data to answer meaningful public health questions across diverse settings. My research combines geospatial analysis, ecological modeling, Earth observation data, and simulation approaches — from forecasting Aedes aegypti abundance in California to building a multi-model ensemble for TBE risk in Germany — to support more practical, data-driven public health action.

More broadly, I am interested in building tools that connect environmental data, surveillance systems, and decision-making in places where vector-borne disease risk is growing.
