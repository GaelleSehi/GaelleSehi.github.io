About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Public Health at the University of California, Irvine, with training in spatial epidemiology, mosquito ecology, and applied modeling. My research focuses on understanding the environmental and spatial drivers of Aedes aegypti and using geospatial and simulation methods to support public health decision-making.

My work integrates ecological modeling, Earth observation data, GIS, and statistical analysis to study mosquito abundance, forecast short-horizon risk, and evaluate vector control strategies such as the sterile insect technique (SIT).

I am especially interested in building practical tools that connect environmental data, surveillance systems, and public health action in California and other settings where vector-borne disease risk is growing.
